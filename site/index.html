SAR flood mapping without Earth Engine

Sentinel-1 flood mapping for Indian events, rebuilt from public archives on a single laptop — classical thresholding and change detection, five segmentation networks trained on Sen1Floods11, InSAR coherence from HyP3, and a parameterised report that regenerates end to end for every event.

3 of 8 events published · source and method on GitHub · Manish Bilore · updated 24 Sep 2026

What came out of it

  • Benchmark IoU does not predict transfer — five networks within 0.663–0.681 test IoU on Sen1Floods11 map flood extents differing by 3–3.5× on the same scene, in both rural events.
  • A ratio of decibel values is not a flood rule. The 2024 Earth Engine logic it replaces puts 79 % of its detections in tree cover, because the drop it demands scales with a pixel's own brightness.
  • Global thresholding fails in dense cities. Over Bengaluru, Otsu lands at −2.6 dB, separating built-up from vegetation rather than water from land, while change detection against a dry-season median still works.
  • Revisit, not method, is the binding constraint for urban pluvial floods. Mumbai 2024 has one orbit at a 12-day repeat and no pass within three days of the cloudburst.
  • Urban flood extent is a lower bound everywhere: intensity rules look for a backscatter drop, while flooded streets get brighter through double bounce.

Every report regenerates from one parameterised Quarto template: raw imagery, speckle filtering, VV against VH, InSAR coherence, terrain and water masks, classical baselines, five deep-learning models, inter-method agreement, a sensitivity analysis of the ensemble, and the limits of each result. Input data is open throughout — Sentinel-1 RTC from the Planetary Computer, SLC bursts through ASF HyP3, Copernicus DEM, JRC Global Surface Water, ESA WorldCover.
